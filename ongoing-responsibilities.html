Keep Your Nonprofit Accountable After You Start

Free learning guide · October 21, 2026 · 4 min read · Nonprofit Wizard

Build a practical calendar for annual reporting, state requirements, board decisions, and financial records.

Make the continuing work visible before it becomes urgent. A shared calendar, an organized document folder, and a short board agenda can prevent small administrative tasks from being forgotten.

Track the filings that apply to your organization

Many exempt organizations must file an annual Form 990-series return or notice. The correct filing depends on the organization's classification, receipts, assets, and applicable exceptions. Churches and some other organizations have different requirements. Review the current IRS guidance rather than assuming every nonprofit files the same form.

Failure to file a required annual return or notice for three consecutive years can cause automatic revocation of exemption. Keep a record of the submission and confirmation, not just a calendar reminder.

Check state responsibilities separately

State corporate reports, charitable fundraising registration, and state tax exemptions are separate topics. Check the offices in each state relevant to your work and solicitation activities. Requirements and exemptions vary.

Keep financial records you can explain

Use organizational accounts and a consistent bookkeeping process. Retain receipts, record restricted gifts appropriately, and establish who approves payments. Give the board information that helps it understand what came in, what was spent, and what the organization has committed to do.

Document the decisions that matter

Keep board minutes, current bylaws, conflict disclosures, major agreements, and government correspondence. Decide who maintains the records and how a replacement officer will gain access if someone leaves.

Review whether the program is working

Choose a few measures appropriate to the activity: sessions delivered, people reached, items distributed, or participant feedback. Discuss what the information suggests and what you should change. A charity's purpose continues beyond receiving an approval letter.

A useful recurring board question: What deadline, financial commitment, or program concern needs a named person and a next action today?
